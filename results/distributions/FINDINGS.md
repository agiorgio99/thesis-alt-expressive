# Distribution analysis — key findings (2026-10-04)

Full tables and figures: `report.md` (generated by `scripts/distribution_analysis.py`).
Real effects: all train songs. Engine effects: 431 pilot pairs (8 per
language × technique), same phone spans. Engine modes: v1 presets; world_v2 /
psola with quartile presets (as in the pilot); **sampled** = per-clip targets
drawn from the whole real distribution (2–98 %); **tails** = draws pushed
toward both tails (γ = 0.6).

## A1–A3. Figures for every technique, outliers, new engines
- `figures/<group>_iqr.png` (vibrato-figure style) and `figures/<group>_full.png`
  (whiskers, every outlier, real 1–99 % band) for all six groups, with v1,
  world_v2 and psola next to the real distribution per language.
- v1 leaves the human range often: 34 % of vibrato-extent values and 33 % of
  pharyngeal high-band aperiodicity values fall outside the real 1–99 % range;
  world_v2 / psola ≤ 4 % for every feature and mode.
- Outliers of the real data are partly estimator artefacts: vibrato rate is
  searched in 3–10 Hz, so values stuck at 3 Hz are failed estimates, not
  singers.

## A4. Diversity
- Quartile presets make the data too uniform: spread of world_v2 is 0.2–0.9 of
  the real spread (central 90 %), e.g. Mixed Voice ΔF0 0.30, Pharyngeal ΔF0 0.20.
- Sampled mode restores most of it: Mixed Voice ΔF0 0.91, Pharyngeal ΔF0 0.68,
  Breathy ΔH1–H2 0.75, vibrato onset 1.0; KS distance to real drops in most
  features (e.g. vibrato extent 0.20 → 0.15, Pharyngeal ΔF0 0.21 → 0.13).
- Between languages, v1 is nearly identical everywhere (ratio 0.1–0.3 on most
  voice-quality features); world_v2 reproduces the real between-language
  differences (ratio ≈ 1).
- Still too uniform in every mode: spectral tilt (0.15–0.5 of real spread),
  because the envelope change is one median ΔLTAS curve per language, only
  scaled. Glide duration is not changed by any engine (fixed by the spans).

## A5. Tails and human range
- Share of values in the real outer deciles (real = 0.20): quartile world_v2
  0.00–0.15 on most features (it never visits the tails); sampled 0.13–0.25;
  tails mode 0.20–0.44 — while staying inside the human range (≤ 4 % outside).
- Recommendation: use **sampled** as the default and **tails** to
  over-represent rare values; both are implemented
  (`world_v2.sample_presets`, `psola.sample_presets`).

## A6. Language, singer, voice type or register?
- Register (pitch tercile within each singer) explains little variance
  (η² ≤ 0.06) but has consistent trends: vibrato extent 57 → 49 cents from low
  to high register; falsetto F0 jump 420 → 327 cents; more high-band
  aperiodicity and a steeper tilt change at higher pitch in breathy / falsetto.
- Voice type dominates the falsetto F0 jump (η² 0.50 vs language 0.28).
- Singer always explains more than language (e.g. vibrato extent 0.33 vs 0.30).

## A7. Do targets rest on one performer?
- Every language has 1–3 singers, and the largest singer contributes 60–100 %
  of the spans of a technique. Removing one singer moves the median target by
  more than 0.5 IQR in many cells (up to 1.6 IQR, French vibrato rate).
- So "language differences" are largely **singer differences** (relevant to the
  cross-cultural bias question). Options: pool targets across languages and
  condition on voice type (and register), or mix language-specific and pooled
  draws. The engines never copy an individual recording (targets are
  distribution quantiles), so there is no per-recording overfitting; the
  risk is per-singer, not per-recording.

## Caveats
- Engine distributions come from 8 clips per cell (pilot pairs); real ones
  from all train songs.
- Pharyngeal: world_v2 raises level by ~2 dB more than real (median 2.7 vs
  0.8 dB) — to check together with the aperiodicity gain fix.
